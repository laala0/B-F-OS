01

Wet concrete burns

Pour days

Wet concrete is strongly alkaline. It can cause chemical burns that don't hurt until hours later, when the damage is already done.

  • Wear waterproof gloves, and boots with your pants over the top, not tucked in.
  • Wear eye protection on the hose, screed and finishing.
  • Don't kneel in wet concrete without waterproof knee protection.
  • Got it on your skin? Wash it off with clean water right away. Soaked clothes? Change them.

Ask the crew: "Who's got spare gloves, and where's the clean water today?"

02

Excavations & trench walls

Dig & footings

A trench wall can let go in seconds, with no warning. A single cubic metre of soil weighs about as much as a small car.

  • Anything over 1.2 m (4 ft) deep that workers go into needs protection: sloping, benching or shoring. Follow WorkSafeBC and your engineer.
  • Keep spoil piles, materials and machines back from the edge.
  • Keep a ladder or safe way out within easy reach. Re-check the walls after rain or vibration.
  • Never work in an excavation alone.

Ask the crew: "If this wall moves, where's our way out?"

03

Rebar: impalement & trips

Forms & steel

Exposed vertical bars and loose tie wire cause some of the worst injuries on concrete sites, and trips and cuts happen every day.

  • Cap or cover exposed vertical bars. Basic caps stop scrapes; where someone could fall onto bars, use impalement-rated caps or wood troughs.
  • Keep walkways clear of offcuts, tie wire and bundles.
  • Wear gloves for handling steel, and get a second person on heavy bundles.

Ask the crew: "Which bars on this site are uncapped right now?"

04

Pump truck, boom & power lines

Pour days

The boom, the hose and the lines overhead can all kill you, and they all move faster than you expect.

  • Never stand under the boom. Know where the overhead lines are, and use a spotter to keep the boom outside WorkSafeBC limits of approach.
  • Hose whip: stand clear of the hose end when pumping starts or stops and when a blockage is cleared.
  • Agree hand signals and a clear stop signal with the operator before the first truck.

Ask the crew: "Who's our spotter, and what's the stop signal?"

05

Silica dust: cutting & grinding

Any day

Cutting, grinding or drilling concrete releases crystalline silica. Breathing it causes silicosis and lung cancer, and the damage is permanent.

  • Cut wet, or use tools with a shroud and a HEPA vacuum.
  • Wear the respirator the job calls for, and make sure it fits.
  • Don't dry-sweep dust; use water or a HEPA vac. Wash up before you eat.
  • Your employer should have a silica exposure control plan. Ask to see it.

Ask the crew: "What are we cutting today, and how are we controlling the dust?"
