Before the pour:
30 checks

Once the concrete is in, every mistake is permanent, and fixing it means a saw, a jackhammer or an engineer's letter. Run this list the day before and again the morning of the pour. Print it and hand it around.

Builders & GCsCrews & foremenEvery trade in the slabHomeowners

1Paperwork & inspectionsNo sign-off, no pour

  • Permit on site, latest approved drawings in hand. Check the revision date. Everyone works off the same set.Builder
  • Required inspections booked and passed. The municipal footing/foundation inspection, plus the engineer's field review where the design calls for one.Builder
  • Layout verified. Setbacks, gridlines and elevations checked against the site plan. A form survey is done if your municipality requires one.Builder
  • Mix ordered to spec. Strength (MPa), exposure class, air content, slump and aggregate size, all matching the drawings.BuilderCrew
  • Volume, start time and truck spacing confirmed with the plant. Order a small overage so you're not short on the last wall.Crew

2FormsBlowouts start where nobody checked

  • Dimensions and diagonals checked. Forms square, plumb and in line with the drawings.
  • Bracing, stakes and walers solid. Walk the full run and push on it.
  • Top of pour marked with grade nails, a chalk line or tape, so the finisher knows where to stop.
  • Ties at the form system's spacing; release agent on the panels.
  • Bulkheads and keyways set wherever the pour stops (planned cold joints).
  • Bottom clean. No mud, standing water, debris, frost or ice. Footings bear on undisturbed or properly compacted soil.

3RebarThe part nobody sees again

  • Size, spacing and laps match the drawings; laps tied.
  • Cover is right. Chairs and dobies hold steel off the ground and off the forms (typically 75 mm where concrete is cast against earth; confirm on your drawings).
  • Dowels and verticals tied in place. No "wet-sticking" unless the engineer allows it.
  • Steel is clean: no mud, oil or loose, flaky rust.
  • Corner bars and extra bars at openings placed as detailed.

4Every trade: what gets buriedGet each trade's OK before the truck

  • Plumbing: sleeves for sewer, water and drain lines through footings and walls. Under-slab rough-in tested and inspected.Plumber
  • Electrical: grounding electrode (concrete-encased / "Ufer") in place if the electrician specified it; conduit sleeves set.Electrician
  • Framing: anchor bolts and hold-downs at the right spacing, embedment and location. Use templates; don't eyeball them.Framer
  • Mechanical & gas: sleeves for lines and venting; radon / soil-gas rough-in where required.HVACGas
  • Drainage: perimeter drain tile plan agreed, and every under-slab or through-wall sleeve in.Builder
  • Openings: beam pockets, window bucks and blockouts in the right place and braced.FramerCrew
  • Photograph everything: rebar, sleeves and embeds, before the pour. When it's buried, the photos are your only record.Everyone

5Site, weather & crewPour day runs on prep

  • Truck and pump access: path clear, ground firm enough, washout spot set. Never wash out into a storm drain.
  • Overhead power lines located. The pump boom stays outside WorkSafeBC limits of approach.
  • Forecast checked. Have a cold-weather plan (blankets, heat) if it's heading under 5 °C, and a curing plan for hot or windy days.
  • Crew briefed: who's on the hose, vibrator, screed and finish. Agree hand signals with the pump operator.
  • Tools ready: a vibrator plus a backup, screeds, floats, water, and lights if it runs late.
  • PPE for wet concrete: waterproof gloves, boots with pants over the top, eye protection. Wet concrete burns skin.

6After the pourBonus

  • Cure and protect. Keep it moist or covered and protected from freezing. Strip forms and backfill only when the engineer or spec says so.
